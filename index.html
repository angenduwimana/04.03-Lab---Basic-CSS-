5 Things to Know About Baby Rattlesnakes

UA College of Pharmacy | August 6, 2014

In Arizona, rattlesnakes can be active during any time of the year. However, baby rattlesnakes are usually born in July and August. This makes summer a more dangerous time for people who spend time outdoors.

Many people in Arizona are bitten by rattlesnakes every year. The Arizona Poison and Drug Information Center helps people across the state by giving free advice over the phone at any time of day.

Poison center workers often talk to people who did not realize they were bitten by a snake. If someone feels a sudden sting or bite while outside, they should call the poison center right away.

Calling quickly can help doctors decide what treatment is needed. Getting help sooner usually leads to better results and faster recovery.

If you may come near rattlesnakes, keep these five facts in mind:

  1. Baby rattlesnakes are small and can hide easily in grass or bushes.
  2. Young rattlesnakes do not have rattles yet, so they make no warning sound.
  3. Even small rattlesnakes can inject dangerous venom.
  4. Adult rattlesnakes do not always rattle before biting.
  5. If you find a strange bite or cut, call the poison center even if it does not hurt.
